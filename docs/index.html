Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

A Gradient Boosting approach to detecting declining search content and generating ranked editorial action recommendations from the FlyRank Search Intelligence Warehouse.

👤 Huzaifah 🔬 Lane 2: Content Refresh Scoring 📅 July 2026

Abstract

Can we automatically identify web pages that are silently losing search visibility before an editor notices? Using 30,000 pseudonymized content records from the FlyRank Search Intelligence Warehouse, I trained a Gradient Boosting classifier to score pages by their probability of experiencing a >20% month-over-month impression decline. The model achieved a Precision@50 of 0.824, nearly doubling the 0.464 baseline established by a composite rule-based scoring system. The output is a ranked content action playbook with reason codes and archetype-to-action mappings, designed for human editorial review. All results are observational and directional — they surface likely declining pages, but do not prove that a refresh will reverse the trend.

Introduction / Problem Statement

Companies invest heavily in content that earns search visibility, but that visibility is fragile. Pages silently decay — their impressions drop, click-through rates erode, and rankings slip — often without anyone noticing until the damage compounds over months.

The decision this work supports is: "Which pages should a content editor review this week for potential refresh?"

A human editor cannot manually monitor thousands of pages. A simple SQL rule (e.g., "flag pages older than 12 months") produces too many false positives. Machine learning helps because search decay is non-linear: a page with 10,000 impressions dropping 5% is a crisis, while a page with 100 impressions dropping 50% is statistical noise. A tree-based model learns these conditional boundaries across thousands of pages simultaneously.

Data

Source: FlyRank ML Internship dataset (Hugging Face, gated access), pseudonymized starter slice refresh_feature_vector.csv.

Grain: One row = one pseudonymized content item (page) at a point-in-time snapshot.

Records: 30,000 pages across multiple anonymized client websites.

Feature window: Current-state signals only (CTR, average position, content age, session volume). All features represent the present or past — no future-window data is used.

Excluded Columns (Leakage Prevention)

The following columns were deliberately excluded from the feature set because they are computed from the same future time window as the label:

  • impressions_last_30d, impressions_prev_30d — direct components of the trend calculation
  • trend_pct — the exact percentage change used to derive the label
  • trend_direction — the label itself

client_id and content_id were used exclusively for grouping and validation splits, never as model features.

Methodology

Label Definition

A page is labeled as declining (1) if trend_direction == 'down', which corresponds to a >20% month-over-month impression decline. All other pages are labeled 0.

Engineered Features

Beyond the raw warehouse columns, I created interaction features to capture non-linear relationships:

  • session_volume_efficiency — sessions per impression, measuring conversion from visibility to engagement
  • stale_age_interaction — content age × inverse freshness, amplifying risk for old content that hasn't been updated
  • ctr_to_pos — CTR relative to position, detecting pages that underperform their ranking slot

Models Compared

ModelRationale

Random ForestEnsemble of decorrelated trees; robust to outliers
Gradient BoostingSequential error correction; excels at non-linear conditional boundaries

Validation Design

5-fold GroupKFold by client_id — This ensures that all pages from a single client appear in either the train set or the test set, never both. This is critical because pages from the same website share structural and topical similarities. A random split would leak client-level patterns and inflate metrics.

Improvement vs Baseline

The Gradient Boosting model's top-50 recommendations correctly identified 41 out of 50 truly declining pages (Precision@50 = 0.824), compared to only 23 out of 50 for the baseline rule (0.464).

Feature Importance

The dominant predictive signals were ctr, avg_position, and the engineered session_volume_efficiency. The tree-based models successfully learned that content_age_days only becomes a risk factor when combined with a declining ctr_to_pos ratio — something the linear baseline rule failed to capture.

Error Analysis

False positives typically occurred on pages with very low overall search volume, where small absolute impression changes produce large percentage swings. This is expected statistical noise and does not compromise the model's utility for high-traffic pages.

Limitations & Honest Framing

Observational, not causal. These predictions identify pages with a high probability of search demand decay. They do not prove that an editorial refresh will reverse the trend — establishing causality would require a controlled A/B experiment on refreshed vs. non-refreshed pages.

  • Directional guidance: The ranked queue is decision-support tooling for human editors, not an automated content modification system.
  • Data scope: Results are based on the pseudonymized starter slice. Performance may vary on different client verticals or time periods.
  • No real-world deployment feedback: The model has not been validated against actual editorial refresh outcomes.
  • Low-volume noise: Pages with fewer than ~200 monthly impressions produce noisy trend signals; the model's precision is strongest on medium-to-high traffic pages.

Ranked Recommendations (Action Playbook)

The pipeline outputs a ranked content action queue (ml10_ranked_queue.csv) with 30,000 scored pages. Each page receives:

  • A decline probability score (0–1) from the Gradient Boosting model
  • A recommended action mapped from content archetype
  • Reason codes explaining the dominant signals (e.g., STALE_CONTENT, PAGE1_LOW_CTR, IMPRESSION_DROP)
  • A priority tier (P1 Urgent, P2 High, P3 Medium)
